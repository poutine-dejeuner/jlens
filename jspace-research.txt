Research directions around the Jacobian-lens global workspace:
spectra, coherence, development, and coupled feature learning
Working note∗
August 21, 2026

Abstract
Anthropic’s Jacobian-lens paper [1] reports that concept directions in the residual stream of
large language models are transported to the output in an approximately context-independent,
linear way across a band of intermediate layers—an “emergent global workspace”—readable
through a corpus-averaged Jacobian J¯ℓ = Ex [∂hfinal /∂hℓ ]. This note collects six interrelated
research directions proposed in a discussion of that paper: (I) the singular-value spectra of J¯ℓ
across depth, including completed measurements on Qwen3.5-4B; (II) transport coherence—
the per-context Jacobian variance—as a workspace diagnostic; (III) a perturbative residualstream interpretation of the lens and its relation to iterative-inference views of transformers;
(IV) the developmental emergence of the workspace across pretraining checkpoints; (V)
coupled attention–MLP feature-learning dynamics as a candidate mechanism for workspace
formation; and (VI) a program connecting the neural feature ansatz to random-matrix theory,
with a three-spectrum experiment relating weights, gradients, and the lens. Concrete next
measurements are listed in Section 8.

Direction I: Spectral characterization of J¯ℓ across depth

Motivation. The paper measures the effective linear dimensionality of WU J¯ℓ per layer on a
frontier model (its Fig. 28d), but no published work computes the full singular-value spectrum
of the fitted lens as a function of depth on open models. The closest existing results are the
“transport-cone geometry” analysis in the independent replication [3] (raw vs. J-transported
effective dimensionality across a GPT-2 124M–Qwen3 14B ladder, including a self-trained negative
control in which transport increases isotropy) and the per-input detached-Jacobian SVDs of [6],
which concern a different object (exact per-context, not corpus-averaged, Jacobians).

Direction II: Transport coherence as a workspace diagnostic

Motivation. Locally, the Jacobian is exact everywhere; what distinguishes the workspace
band is not that linearization works but that averaging does—feature directions are transported
to the output in a context-independent way, so per-context Jacobians agree and their mean is
meaningful. The natural order parameter is the per-context variance Σℓ in (3), or the scalar
coherence fraction κℓ in (4). The workspace hypothesis predicts κℓ low in sensory layers, rising
sharply at the workspace onset (the paper’s “ignition” layer), and near one in the motor band.

Direction III: The perturbative residual-stream interpretation

Framing. By (2), the J-lens is “logit lens plus resummed first-order corrections”: the logit lens
works late because few near-identity factors remain, and fails early because the accumulated
product has rotated the basis. The residual architecture makes the expansion around the identity
natural, and the notebook’s aℓ I + Rℓ decomposition quantifies it directly (∥R∥ falling with depth
while a → 1). The refinement developed in discussion: the early/middle boundary is not where a
Taylor expansion starts converging (linearization is locally exact everywhere) but where averaged
linearization starts working, i.e., where transport becomes context-independent (Section 3). The
genuinely novel content of the workspace paper sits in that amendment.
Prior work. The view of residual layers as small iterative updates has three strands. Iterative
inference / unrolled estimation: residual blocks as incremental refinement [8, 9], ResNets as
ensembles of shallow paths [10]. Continuous-depth and dynamical-systems views: neural ODEs
[11], transformers as multi-particle systems [12], and the interacting-particle analysis of attention
flows [13]. Interpretability-side evidence: the logit lens [14] and tuned lens [15] are premised on
iterative inference; linear cross-layer transport maps [16]; robustness of middle layers to deletion
and swapping, with a stage decomposition (detokenization / feature engineering / prediction
3

␌ensembling / residual sharpening) that maps onto the sensory/workspace/motor bands [17]; and
the related layer-pruning and shared-basis observations of [18, 19]. On the spectral-theory side,
[7] proves depth-induced separation of ordered singular values in products of masked linear maps
and the resulting forced alignment of singular bases—the formal machinery for when the averaged
J¯ℓ is a well-conditioned transport map.

Direction IV: Developmental emergence across pretraining
checkpoints

Motivation. All work above concerns trained models. No published study fits the J-lens, or
tracks the workspace signatures, across a pretraining checkpoint ladder; the paper itself contains
only a two-point base-vs-post-trained comparison. Dense public checkpoint suites (Pythia [20],
OLMo [21]) make the longitudinal study cheap: the community cost analyses indicate a usable
lens fits on tens of prompts, so a full 154-checkpoint Pythia ladder is modest compute.
Adjacent checkpoint results. Several existing studies cover components of the story. Checkpointwise hidden-state displacement analysis on Pythia and OLMo shows the final layer acquires its
large “jump” during training [22]—i.e., the small-update middle / large-update terminal asymmetry of the perturbative picture is developmental, not architectural. Layer-wise representation
metrics across Pythia checkpoints show intermediate layers change most during training while
early layers stabilize quickly [23]. Crosscoder-based tracking finds a middle-layer representational
phase transition in Pythia-1B between 128M and 4B tokens [24]. The tuned-lens paper correlates
the training iteration at which an example is learned with its inference-time prediction depth on
Pythia-12B [15], suggesting shared refinement structure between the depth and training axes.
Complementary spectral and norm statistics across checkpoints appear in [25] (the residual
stream’s identity-preservation property holds from initialization—so the skip-path backbone
is architectural even though the band structure is learned), in EleutherAI’s training-dynamics
observations (outlier norms stabilizing early; circuit SVD directions converging late) [26], in Ai2’s
OLMo magnitude study [27], and in developmental circuit-formation studies [28].
Proposed measurement. Fit the lens per checkpoint on Pythia-1B (or OLMo for longerhorizon maintenance), and plot as a function of both depth and training step: the CKA block
structure, readout kurtosis, effective dimensionality of WU J¯ℓ , the coherence fraction κℓ (Section 3),
the spectral quantities of Section 2 (qeff drainage, power-law exponent, spike count), and the
MLP-gain curve (Section 6). Predictions: the three-block CKA structure is absent at early
checkpoints; the workspace band crystallizes in or near the 128M–4B-token window of the
crosscoder transition; the early-layer power-law exponent starts near the Fuss–Catalan value
(uncorrelated factors) and falls toward the trained value ≈ 0.45 as alignment develops; and the
coherence fraction’s depth profile sharpens from flat to sigmoidal.

Direction V: Coupled attention–MLP feature-learning dynamics

Motivation. The workspace paper finds that MLP blocks amplify J-lens directions from
preceding attention blocks by roughly an order of magnitude—evidence of weight-space alignment
4

␌between what attention writes and what MLPs read. Two distinct coupling mechanisms appear
in the theory literature and should be distinguished. (i) Loss-mediated competition: subcircuits independent in parameter space, coupled only through explained-away loss, as in the
differential-learning-kinetics analysis of the memorization/generalization (IWL/ICL) transition
[29], its precursor on abrupt induction-head learning [30], and the associative-memory account
of induction-circuit formation with fast MLP bigram statistics and slow attention circuits [31];
see also [32, 33, 34, 35]. (ii) Weight-space alignment: genuinely coupled gradient dynamics,
as in JoMA, which integrates out the attention logits to obtain modified MLP dynamics and
predicts sparse-then-dense attention and hierarchical composition, verified on Pythia and OPT
[36]; provable joint attention–MLP dynamics in knowledge-storage settings [37]; and solvable
co-evolution models for retrieval-plus-update computations [38].
The alignment strand. The MLP-gain finding is an in-the-wild instance of alignment phenomena studied in feature-learning theory: coupled singular-mode dynamics in deep linear networks,
where balancedness forces adjacent layers to share singular vectors [39]; the silent-alignment
effect, where kernels align to task structure before the loss moves [40]; and the neural feature
ansatz (NFA), the empirical law that a trained layer’s weight Gram matrix aligns with the
average gradient outer product (AGOP) of the network with respect to that layer’s input [41],
with derived consequences such as deep neural collapse [42]. In lens language: the AGOP is
the second moment E[J ⊤ J] whose first moment the lens fits, so NFA predicts precisely that
downstream weights preferentially read high-average-Jacobian directions—the measured MLP
gain.
Interpretive mapping and proposed measurement. A tidy (speculative) synthesis: the
alignment mechanism explains how a broadcast subspace forms (mutual read/write amplification
along shared directions in the rich regime), while the competition mechanism explains which
computations end up inside it—practiced, high-frequency computations compile into direct MLP
lookups outside the broadcast channel (the paper’s “automatic” pathway; IWL-like), while
computations requiring flexible routing remain attention-mediated within it (ICL-like). ICL
transience [34] then reads as a rehearsed computation migrating out of the workspace. Proposed
measurement, joining Direction IV: track the MLP-gain curve and broadcast-head statistics per
checkpoint alongside an IWL/ICL behavioral probe. Predictions: gain starts near 1 and grows
concentrated in the emerging workspace band; the two-timescale results [31, 36] fix an ordering
(MLP associative content before read/write alignment); and in small trainable models the gain
growth rate is modulated by data diversity K, per [29].

Direction VI: Neural feature ansatz meets random-matrix
theory

The program. Taking expectations of the NFA relates the spectrum of the weights to the
spectrum of the Jacobian: W ⊤ W ∝ E[J ⊤ J] = J¯⊤ J¯ + Σ. The weight spectrum therefore
dominates the lens spectrum in the PSD order, with gap exactly the per-context covariance
Σ—so the weight–lens correspondence holds conditionally on coherence, i.e., inside the workspace.
RMT enters through a signal-plus-noise model J(x) = J¯ + ∆J(x): with approximately isotropic
fluctuations, E[J ⊤ J] is a spiked (deformed MP) covariance, predicting workspace directions
appear as outliers above the bulk of the weight Gram matrix iff their coherent strength exceeds
5

␌the BBP threshold [47, 46, 45] set by the fluctuation variance, with outlier count tracking
workspace dimensionality. Two structural caveats identified in discussion: trained weight spectra
are often heavy-tailed rather than clean MP [48], favoring eigenvector-overlap statistics over
eigenvalue fits; and freeness—required for free-convolution tools—fails maximally in the aligned
subspace, since NFA is the statement that training aligns W with the downstream Jacobian. The
bulk-plus-non-free-spike structure is therefore forced, not a modeling convenience. Substituting
the factorization (2) into NFA layer by layer yields a closed system of coupled spectral fixed-point
equations, whose ingredients exist in embryo: exact deep-Jacobian spectra at initialization via
free multiplicative convolution [49], dynamical mean-field theories of feature learning through
training [43, 44], and the AGOP-derived collapse results [42].

Summary of proposed measurements

In rough priority order, using the existing Qwen3.5-4B artifacts and public checkpoint suites.
First, the full-depth coherence curve κℓ from the four per-shard lenses, testing for a sharp onset
against a gradual drift, together with the eigenstructure of Σℓ . Second, the spike-overlap analysis:
the overlap matrix between the top ∼ 100 eigenvectors of J¯ℓ⊤ J¯ℓ and the interpretable J-space
directions, resolving whether the flat-in-depth spike population is the workspace. Third, the
band-boundary test: whether the qeff = 1 crossover (layers 18–22 on Qwen3.5-4B) coincides with
behaviorally determined workspace/motor boundaries. Fourth, the three-spectrum NFA test of
Section 7. Fifth, the developmental study of Section 5: lens fits per Pythia/OLMo checkpoint,
tracking band structure, coherence, spectral exponents, and MLP gain, with the data-diversity
modulation experiment of Section 6 in small trainable models. Throughout, the early-layer
power-law exponent serves as a scalar alignment order parameter (effective free-factor count),
cheap to compute at every (layer, checkpoint) pair.
